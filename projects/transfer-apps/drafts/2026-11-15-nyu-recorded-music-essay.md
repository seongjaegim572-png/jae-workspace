Loneliness has followed me for most of my life. It was loudest at the University of Washington, far from my family in Korea, where most nights I sat alone in my room with FL Studio open. Then I joined the Electronic Music Producers club and met a friend, the producer Flaaroni, who felt the same way. Together we turned that feeling into "Loneliness Epidemic," the title track and opening song of my 2024 album.

Making the song taught me why I make music: to turn something heavy into something other people can feel with me. Releasing it taught me the second half of the job. A song does not find its audience by itself. I shot and edited my own promo Reels for it. One reached [N] views from an account with only 150 followers. In 2024 I released a new song every Friday for two months, and my music passed 200,000 Spotify streams with no label and no team.

Since then I have kept building both sides. "With You With You" is the best-made song I have written. It is one of the few tracks I built around vocal samples, and they came together with warm piano and smooth drums. On the business side, I worked as a University Growth Specialist at Suno, making content about how AI tools can help people create music. I grew my family's clinic's Threads to 258,000 views in 30 days, and two of my own Reels passed one million views each.

But I have reached the limit of learning alone. My songs are good, but I want them to be great, and I want to know how to build a real career around them, not just a viral moment.

That is why the Clive Davis Institute is the right place for me. It is the only program I have found that treats making music and building a business around it as one skill, which is exactly how I have been working. In [Course 1] I could push my production and songwriting further, and in [Course 2] I could learn how artists own their work and grow a team. I want to learn from [Faculty name], whose work on [topic] connects to [my goal]. And New York would give me what Seattle and Korea could not: artists to collaborate with every week and stages to test new songs on.

Clive Davis students like Maggie Rogers have shown that a song made in a classroom can reach the world. I want to be the next artist who makes the song and knows how to get it heard.
