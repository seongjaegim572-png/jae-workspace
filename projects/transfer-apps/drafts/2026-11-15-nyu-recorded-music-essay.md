My freshman year at the University of Washington was one of the loneliest times of my life. I was far from home in Korea, and most nights I ended up alone in my room with FL Studio open. Then I joined the Electronic Music Producers club and met a friend who felt the same way. Together we turned that feeling into a song, "Loneliness Epidemic." It became the title track of my first album.

Making the song taught me why I make music: to turn something heavy into something other people can feel with me. Releasing it taught me the second half of the job. A song does not find its audience by itself. I shot and edited my own promo Reels for it and kept going. In 2024 I released a new song every Friday for two months, and my music passed 200,000 Spotify streams with no label and no team.

Since then I have kept building both sides. "With You With You" is the best-made song I have written: [one or two real details, e.g. what you changed in the mix, beat, or hook]. On the business side, I worked as a University Growth Specialist at Suno, making content about how AI tools can help people create music. I grew my family's clinic's Threads to 258,000 views in 30 days, and two of my own Reels passed one million views each.

But I have reached the limit of learning alone. My songs are good, but I want them to be great, and I want to know how to build a real career around them, not just a viral moment.

That is why the Clive Davis Institute is the right place for me. It is the only program I have found that treats making music and building a business around it as one skill, which is exactly how I have been working. In [Course 1] I could push my production and songwriting further, and in [Course 2] I could learn how artists own their work and grow a team. I want to learn from [Faculty name], whose work on [topic] connects to [my goal]. And New York would give me what Seattle and Korea could not: artists to collaborate with every week and stages to test new songs on.

Clive Davis students like Maggie Rogers have shown that a song made in a classroom can reach the world. I want to be the next artist who makes the song and knows how to get it heard.
